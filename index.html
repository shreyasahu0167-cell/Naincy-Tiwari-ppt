Introduction

  • Safe drinking water is a fundamental requirement for public health and educational institutions.
  • School children are highly vulnerable to water-borne diseases.
  • Variations in water quality often exist due to institutional and geographical disparities.
  • This study focuses on a comparative assessment of:
    • Government vs Private Schools
    • Urban vs Rural Areas

Statement of the Problem

  • Unequal access to safe drinking water in schools.
  • Lack of proper monitoring and maintenance systems.
  • Higher risk of contamination in rural and government institutions.
  • Need for systematic comparative evaluation.

Objectives of the Study

  • To assess drinking water quality in selected schools.
  • To compare quality between:
    • Government and Private Schools
    • Urban and Rural Areas
  • To evaluate water based on standard parameters.
  • To identify key issues and suggest improvements.

Study Area & Methodology

Study Area

Location: Shuklaganj

Divided into:

  • Urban zone (town region)
  • Rural zone (village region)

Sample includes: Government schools and Private schools.

Research Methodology

  • Research Design: Comparative & Analytical
  • Data Collection: Primary (Water sample testing) & Secondary (Reports, standards)
  • Sampling Technique: Random Sampling
  • Analysis Tool: Water Quality Index (WQI)

Parameters of Analysis

  • Physical Parameters: Colour, Odour, Turbidity
  • Chemical Parameters: pH, Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), Hardness
  • Biological Parameters: Coliform bacteria, E. coli presence

Results

Government Schools

Rural: High turbidity and microbial contamination. Absence of purification systems.

Urban: Moderate water quality. Basic filtration facilities present.

Private Schools

Urban: Better water quality. Use of RO/UV purification systems.

Rural: Moderate quality. Limited infrastructure.

Key Findings & Discussion

Key Findings

  • Significant disparity in water quality across categories.
  • Urban schools perform better than rural schools.
  • Private schools show better compliance with standards.
  • Rural government schools are most vulnerable.
  • Lack of regular monitoring is a major concern.

Discussion

  • Quality variation is influenced by economic resources, institutional management, and infrastructure.
  • Rural areas face groundwater contamination issues.
  • Government schools lack proper maintenance systems.
  • Seasonal variation (monsoon) increases contamination.

Conclusion & Suggestions

Conclusion

  • Drinking water quality is uneven across schools.
  • Private institutions maintain better standards.
  • Rural government schools need urgent intervention.
  • Ensuring safe water is critical for student health and learning outcomes.

Suggestions

  • Installation of RO/UV systems in all schools.
  • Regular water quality monitoring.
  • Government funding for rural infrastructure.
  • Awareness programs on hygiene and sanitation.
  • Periodic cleaning of storage tanks.

References
